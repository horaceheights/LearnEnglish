# A1+ activity proposals for user decision

Decision recorded 2026-10-26: the user approved conversation decisions and chose existing single images or separate spoken turns instead of timed photo sequences. The approved v9 communicative outcomes remain the content authority. The text below preserves the concrete alternatives reviewed; section 1 is approved, section 2's new mechanic is not approved.

## 1. Conversation decisions inside a continuous mission

The current mission requires every visible target to receive its own cue and be solved. That works for finding people or objects, but does not represent choosing one useful question or one workable plan. Replacing the latter with identifying a speaker would lose the intended comprehension task.

Proposed shared activity: play the exact English situation, then unlock two or three neutral, randomized English alternatives. One correct selection advances the conversation and plays its authored response. A wrong selection gives the shared, specific explanation and keeps the same story state. No Comprobar button, tiles, lives, speed requirement, or five-section lesson shell. The existing four consecutive closing pronunciation gates and unscored completion scene remain.

Concrete missing-information example, using language taught before the mission:

- Heard: Ana says, “I study at home at seven.”
- Choices: “What time do you study?” / “How often do you study at home?”
- Correct: the frequency question, because the time is already stated.
- After selection: Luis asks the chosen question; Ana answers, “I usually study at home.”
- Visible Spanish setup introduces the conversation only; it never names frequency as the missing fact.

Concrete changed-plan example:

- Earlier English establishes an eight o'clock meeting and willingness to meet.
- Heard update: “Ana usually studies at home, but today she is studying at the library.”
- Choices: “Let's meet at home at eight.” / “Let's meet at the library at eight.”
- Correct: library. Luis makes that proposal and Ana accepts once. The story then moves to preparation and arrival, without replaying the same agreement as another assessed beat.

This requires explicitly superseding the existing no-answer-bank rule for this reusable mission activity. It does not approve arbitrary per-card Spanish questions or answer-revealing help.

## 2. Ordered photos that show an action changing state

The current shared still-image contract permits one image per spoken turn. The candidate instead uses timed multi-image sequences, including silent recognition prompts; that mechanism has not been approved.

Proposed shared activity: an authored, replayable before/action/after sequence in one unchanged image viewport. Choices unlock after the complete sequence is visible. All images preserve the same person, clothes, setting and objects. Replay repeats that same sequence; it does not shuffle frames or play the correct answer. Both clients use the same sequence data and lifecycle.

Concrete example: shoes off → Ana putting shoes on → shoes on. The learner chooses “I put on my shoes.” or “I take off my shoes.” Only a correct choice plays the selected sentence. The sequence supplies observable direction, rather than asking the learner to infer it from a still showing hands near a shoe.

If this standard is not approved, replace such tasks with unambiguous single action pictures or existing separate audio turns where that preserves the assessment. Do not silently split an assessment into unrelated cards or declare an ambiguous single still sufficient.

## Decisions unaffected by these proposals

Shared instructions, no answer leakage, exact immutable audio, existing clocks and pronunciation delivery, chronological continuity, contextual feedback, and human review of all new media remain required. These proposals do not approve publication or mark any generated asset human-reviewed.
