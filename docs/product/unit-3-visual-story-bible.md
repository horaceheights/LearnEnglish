# Unit 3 Visual Story Bible

Last reviewed: 2026-08-30

This file defines the human and narrative continuity for Unit 3 still media. It supplements the semantic scene contracts in `a1-course-canvas.json`; the complete card prompt and option contract remain authoritative.

## Learner-first rule

- Every human concept is a photorealistic real-world scene with real people, natural body language, and enough visible context to understand the meaning at first glance.
- Judge the image as a first-time learner would. Filenames, private production notes, text bubbles, halos, arrows, and floating teaching icons cannot carry the meaning.
- Preserve continuity while the learner is following the same people or event. Start a visibly new context with new people whenever that is more natural, clearer, or more interesting.
- The course should feel populated. Use varied adults, families, workers, customers, travelers, and neighbors across unrelated contexts instead of forcing the anchor cast into every example.

## Anchor characters

### Ana

- Established appearance: Latina young adult with long dark wavy hair; green overshirt, white top, jeans, and cream shoulder tote in the survey sequence.
- Established role in the opening sequence: community volunteer conducting a public neighborhood survey with a clipboard and a small courtyard or community-center station.
- Established relationship to Luis: respondent and new acquaintance, not a housemate, partner, neighbor, or family member.
- Curriculum identity used only when a card requires it: name Ana, age answer 20, from Mexico/Mexican, teacher, owns a book.
- Her later teacher scene is an explicit new workplace/classroom context. It does not retroactively turn the survey station into her home or school.

### Luis

- Established appearance: Latino young adult with short dark curly hair; navy T-shirt and khaki trousers in the survey sequence; dark work jacket, blue shirt, messenger bag, and coffee at the later bus stop.
- Established role in the opening sequence: survey respondent.
- Established relationship to Ana: respondent and new acquaintance.
- Curriculum identity used only when a card requires it: name Luis, age answer 18, from the United States/American, driver, owns a car.
- His later driver and car scenes are explicit new work/ownership contexts rather than unexplained changes at the survey station.

### Sofia and Diego

- Introduce Sofia and Diego as new real people in the international-community sequence; preserve each identity after introduction.
- Sofia's required identity is Canadian and the named owner of a car or bike where the card explicitly calls for her. Do not reuse her as both the generic cook and generic nurse.
- Diego's required identity is Spanish and a doctor where the card explicitly calls for him.

## Approved opening sequence

1. Ana arrives and says hello; the doorway and body direction make arrival explicit.
2. At the public survey station, Ana points to her badge/clipboard and asks Luis's name.
3. Ana identifies herself by pointing directly to her `ANA` badge.
4. Ana and Luis complete reciprocal name exchanges with the active speaker and respondent visible through gaze and gesture.
5. The next morning, they recognize each other at a public bus stop while both carry coffee. Morning light and the clock establish `Good morning`.
6. Luis boards the arriving bus while Ana remains at the stop. Direction of travel makes `Goodbye` explicit.

## Pronoun continuation

- Return to the same survey event for `I`, `you`, and `we` because speaker perspective benefits from an established pair.
- `I`: Ana points naturally to herself while Luis listens.
- `You`: Ana directs a natural gesture toward Luis while he performs the target action.
- `We`: Ana visibly includes herself and Luis in one natural gesture while both participate in the target action.
- No bubbles, halos, arrows, labels, or split panels. Gaze, hands, action, and spatial direction must do the teaching.

## New-context patterns

- `Am/is/are`: use fresh, natural household, workplace, or recreation scenes; one answer concept per image. Never use the old he/she split panel.
- Numbers 11-20: use a consistent real public registration or community-event pattern with varied participants holding one clear physical numbered card. The person and setting make it a scenario; the large physical numeral remains readable at mobile size.
- Countries and nationalities: use a real international welcome desk, community fair, travel desk, or map interaction with varied people. Flags and maps may support the meaning but cannot replace the person and scenario. Avoid ethnic stereotypes.
- Professions: use different real adults in unmistakable workplaces with visible work actions and tools. Do not assign unrelated generic jobs to Ana or Sofia merely because an internal filename contains their name.
- Possession: show a natural ownership or handoff action. The owner, speaker/listener relationship, and object must be clear without floating ownership icons.
- Unit review and mission: return to Ana and Luis only for their required personal profiles and exchanges. Integrate profile facts into believable photographs or a coherent sequence rather than synthetic profile-card collages.

## Visual production pattern

- 3:2 landscape, 1536x1024.
- Documentary/editorial photorealism, natural light and anatomy, mobile-safe central composition.
- Favor distinct locations, wardrobe, ages, and faces between unrelated scenes.
- Keep only expendable background near the outer crop edges; hands, faces, teaching actions, quantities, and ownership links stay within the safe area.
- Minimal readable text is allowed only when it is a physical part of the scenario and the concept depends on it, such as `ANA`, `LUIS`, or one numbered registration card.
