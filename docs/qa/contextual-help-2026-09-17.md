# Contextual help verification

The reported regression is Lesson 1.1 U2: submitting `boy`, `a` for `He is ___ ___.` previously produced an article-sound explanation with an empty noun. Full construction separately revealed a word number without teaching its relationship.

Both clients now use the construction teaching resolver for both completion forms. The resolver compares the actual attempt with the accepted sequence, locates the first meaningful mismatch, and chooses the smallest supported relationship containing the confused word occurrences. Canonical scaffold words and punctuation remain available when explaining adjacent blanks. Accepted identical words compare by label. Existing choice-specific hints remain active for ordinary recognition/listening/grammar choices.

Wrong constructions retain their submitted words and hint until the explicit `Reintentar` button resets this attempt. Word input is locked during feedback; retry preserves score and first-try history. Listening first-use help and the help button share the approved ear-training instruction and speaker replay copy.

## Automated and semantic review

- The construction inventory contains 459 Use cards across the complete 70-lesson catalog. Every slot requires a supported explanation, and the reconstructed target must match the answer audio.
- The guardrail exercises all legal pair swaps, reversals and rotations, including later mistakes and duplicate-word equivalence. Unsupported future patterns fail before release.
- Focused regressions cover the screenshot, subject/action versus action/object in the same sentence, article/name, quantity/color/name, question inversion, negation, time phrases, `listen to`, `want to`, polite formulas, partial attempts and second-sentence mistakes.
- Content stays in the canonical lesson files; no lesson, answer, image, audio or scoring contract is regenerated by this change.

Representative reviewed explanations:

| Context | Mistake | Relationship taught |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1: `He is a boy.` | `boy a` | `a` accompanies the name `boy` and goes before it. |
| Unit 2: `Two blue cars.` | color/name swapped | Quantity, then color, then the thing named. |
| Unit 3: `How old are you?` | `old How` | `How old` asks age; `How` precedes `old`. |
| Unit 4: `The book is on the table.` | `table the` | `the` precedes the thing named; the location remains the context. |
| Unit 5: `I like apples.` | `apples like` | What is liked follows `like`, distinct from who likes it. |
| Unit 6: `The bus leaves at eight.` | `eight at` | `at` introduces the departure time and goes before it. |
| Unit 7: `Do you want to read?` | `read to` | `to` joins `want` to the proposed activity and precedes that verb. |
